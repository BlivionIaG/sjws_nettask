## #415: what changes (drafted on this branch)

1. **Lean consumer** (`StreamLoad --lean-consumer`, default on for binary; `LEAN_CONSUMER=0` turns it off).
   The `:stream` POST and the `replies` GET go over one raw keep-alive socket per consumer. The chunked body is
   de-chunked in place (`ChunkedIn`), frames are read into one reused buffer, and stamps are parsed from bytes.
   This removes `HttpClient` (selector thread hand-off, per-read `ByteBuffer` lists, `ofInputStream` queue)
   and the per-frame `byte[]` + `String` + `Long.parseLong` from the hottest generator path. `:stream` uses
   `postOnce` (no resend, so a reconnect can't open a second session).
2. **Leaner producer.** The data-frame request head is built once per stream (path, token and length are
   fixed) instead of string-concatenated per POST. Responses are parsed from bytes (status digits,
   case-insensitive header match in place) instead of `String.split` / `toLowerCase` per header line.
3. **Pacing** (`--rate-mib-s R` / `RATE_MIB_S`, unset = today's closed loop). Each stream sends one chunk
   every `streams × 1024 / (R × 2^20)` s (≈ 8.9 ms per stream at R = 110), random phase. Late slots are caught
   up, not dropped (wrk2-style). Each chunk carries its actual send stamp at data[0..20) and its **scheduled
   send time** at data[20..40). **Paced windows report `p99_us` from the scheduled send time**
   (`latency_basis: "scheduled"`, ruling Q1), so a stalled producer or a server that falls behind can't hide
   backlog. Closed loop keeps the send stamp (identical there). `p99_send_us` and `p99_sched_us`
   (`chunk_latency_send` / `chunk_latency_sched` with histograms) are always reported side by side, with
   `offered_mib_s`, `sched_late_pct` and `sched_lag_max_us`. Paced runs need `chunk_bytes ≥ 40`. **No
   validity rule changes:** a producer behind schedule because the server is slow is a server result, not an
   invalid window.
3b. **Gate rule (ruling Q1; Q2 per inceptor's ruling of 2026-11-11 ~23:44, see *Rulings*; disclosed on PR 8 and
   in #502; Architecture sign-off pending).**
   - **Disclosure: the bar moves from p99 at closed-loop saturation to p99 at a fixed 110 MiB/s offered
     operating point.** The gate number is `p99_us` on the scheduled-send basis (Q2, inceptor ruling
     2026-11-11 ~23:44; Architecture's position agrees). Thresholds are unchanged.
   - **Rate pinned.** `gate.sh` runs every abs and A/A window paced at exactly `GATE_RATE_MIB_S=110`. `agg.py`
     rejects abs/A-A windows at any other offered rate (100, 105 and 120 are all rejected, not just < 100),
     and any window not on the scheduled basis. A/A legs off 110 also fail the verdict.
   - **Pass rule.** Median achieved ≥ 100 MiB/s and median p99 < 1 ms. Guards are unchanged (≥ 80 MiB/s,
     p99 ≤ 1.5 ms).
   - **Actual offered rate.** Every window reports `offered_actual_mib_s = sched_sends × chunk_bytes / secs`
     next to the target. `sched_sends` counts send **starts** of in-window slots (one per slot, retries not
     counted, late catch-up sends included). `sched_sent` (2xx answers) is reported separately. `agg.py`
     fails a window whose `offered_actual_mib_s` is more than **0.5 %** from 110. Edge rounding is at most
     one slot per stream in about 2 250 (≤ 0.05 %), so 0.5 % is ten times that, and it still catches about
     1 % of slots never sent.
   - **Why 110, a 10 % margin.** With slot-based accounting (below), achieved ≈ offered unless chunks are lost.
     So 110 offered means the p99 is measured at 1.1× the throughput bar, which is stricter than measuring
     at 100. The margin also absorbs phase and window-edge rounding (one slot per stream is ≤ 0.1 % of a
     20 s window), so a lossless window cannot drop below 100 by accident. It is not higher because the
     generator budget already binds: 14.2 µs/chunk at 110 in mode (A), with 25 µs the best measured so far.
     More margin would make the window self-loaded rather than stricter.
   - **Window accounting (Architecture item 1).**
     - Samples are counted by **slot (scheduled) time** inside `[start, end)`, not by receive time.
     - **Slot schedules are anchored at the wave epoch** (before the admit ramp), not at the claim return
       (re-review item 1). Every registered stream owns a full window of expected slots whenever its
       claim returned. A claim returning mid-window or after the window starts at the first slot ≥ start
       and catches up, so those slots show their real lateness. If it can't finish by the cutoff, the rest
       is aged.
     - The window start is published 50 ms before it opens. Producers read it after reading the clock,
       so a claim returning just as the window opens can't miss its first slots.
     - **Unmeasured slots are never caught up.** In warmup, and for measure-wave slots before the window
       opens, a late producer skips to its next slot. A backlog there can't burst into the window's first
       seconds.
     - Producers stop at their first slot ≥ end. Consumers keep receiving through the drain cutoff,
       `end + max(drain timeout, 75 s)`, and sockets are closed at the cutoff.
     - Every in-window slot still unsent or unreceived at cutoff is recorded in the scheduled histogram at
       its age, `cutoff − slot`. A stream that never claimed a session counts all its window slots. Missing
       slots are taken as the stream's youngest ones, so each recorded age is a **lower bound** on the true
       age. That is why `agg.py` never reports a p99 for a window with any shortfall: it prints
       `p99=n/a (shortfall N)`.
     - Reported per window: `sched_expected`, `sched_sent`, `sched_received`, `sched_unreceived_at_cutoff`,
       `sched_phantom_streams`, `sched_streams_short` (streams with any missing slot), `cutoff_hit`,
       `catchup_s` and `catchup_cpu_pct_of_wall` (generator CPU from the window end until the producers
       finish; `agg.py` fails a window saturated for ≥ 1 s of catch-up).
     - `agg.py` fails any shortfall (`sched_received < sched_expected`), any `cutoff_hit`, or a missing
       accounting field, and any stream short of its slots (`sched_streams_short > 0`). **The allowed
       shortfall is 0** (Architecture accepted this). Because we keep receiving until the cutoff, nothing
       legitimate is still in flight then; `sched_inflight_at_end` is reported for information only.
   - **Schedule lag (item 5).** `sched_lag_p50_us` and `sched_lag_p99_us` (actual send − slot) are reported
     on every paced window.
   - **Scheduled-stamp fallbacks** are counted (`sched_stamp_fallback`). Any fallback flags a paced window
     invalid.
   - **Closed-loop info legs (item 4).** The `cl-NN` legs run **after the whole abs phase**, not between abs
     windows, so they cannot raise load1 for the next window. Every leg first waits for quiet: load1 < 3, at
     most 900 s per leg, 5400 s in total, then abort (exit 6). They are printed beside each window and never
     counted. `CLOSED_LOOP_INFO=0` skips them.
   - **Low-load end-to-end floor (item 5, ruling Q5).** `lowload-floor-N` runs at `FLOOR_RATE_MIB_S=10`. It is
     reported beside every window and never subtracted.
     - **Acceptance, fixed in advance:** a valid floor leg with p99 ≤ **300 µs**. If the floor is above that,
       the run cannot resolve the 1 ms gate and the verdict is FAIL.
     - An invalid floor leg is rerun, up to `FLOOR_TRIES=3`.
     - It **understates generator delay at 110 MiB/s**, because at 10 MiB/s the generator is nearly idle.
       Schedule lag at 110 is the in-window measure of generator delay.
   - **Sequencing (item 7, re-review item 2).** No gate window counts until the Q4 validity change lands.
     - Every `result.json` carries `harness_commit` and `validity_marker`. `StreamLoad.VALIDITY_MARKER` is
       `"pre-q4"` until the post-#465 merge. That commit sets it to `"q4"` and sets `agg.Q4_LANDED = True`.
     - Before then `agg.py` prints `VERDICT: NOT_COUNTABLE (pre-Q4 harness, would be X)`.
     - After it, any abs, A/A or floor leg that lacks a harness commit or carries a marker other than `q4`
       is invalid. An old window can never become a PASS.
     - A window without `harness_commit` is always a FAIL reason.
     - `--assume-q4` exists for the tests only and prints `TEST_ONLY_PASS` / `TEST_ONLY_FAIL`, never
       `PASS`.
4. **Histogram export.** `chunk_latency.hist` (sparse LatBag buckets) plus `hist_sub_bits`, so (B)'s
   multi-process windows can be merged exactly rather than by averaging p99s.
5. `gate.sh` defaults `LOADGEN=java` (one.sh's default is the CPython `drive.py`, which can't drive 1k).
   `one.sh` gains `RATE_MIB_S`, `LEAN_CONSUMER`, `SERVER_JAVA_OPTS`, `GEN_JAVA_OPTS` pass-throughs, all empty by
   default so a gate window is unchanged unless set.

     11th stream. Expect a full window of expected slots for those streams, p99 > 1 ms and `offered_actual`
     ≈ the rate.
   - c. **Mid-window claim:** the same delay set to half the window. Expect catch-up from the window start,
     with p99 failing.
   - d. **Hung stream:** a consumer that stops reading. Expect `cutoff_hit`, `sched_streams_short > 0` and
     `p99=n/a`.
   - e. **Warmup at 110 MiB/s on 32 streams:** `sched_lag_p99_us` in the first measure second is no higher
     than in the rest (no catch-up burst).
   - f. **`LEAN_CONSUMER=0` with a hung stream:** the HttpClient readers are closed at the cutoff, and the
     run ends by about the cutoff plus 5 s.
